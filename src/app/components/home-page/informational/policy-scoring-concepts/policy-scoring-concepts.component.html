Scoring Concepts

Multi-layered approaches to policy evaluation and solution development

Policy Scoring Flow

Evaluate and score drafted legislation through expert annotation and public engagement.

Expert-Level Phases

These phases require specialized knowledge and are performed by legal professionals, policy specialists, and domain experts.

gavel Phase 1: Expert Drafting Foundation Layer

Experts (legal professionals, policy specialists) directly draft legislation and propose it as actionable policy. This forms the base document that will be scored and evaluated through subsequent phases.

  • Draft legislation text
  • Propose as formal policy
  • Establish the document for scoring
 highlight Phase 2: Assertion Round Annotation Layer

Once policy is drafted, participants highlight segments of text and make assertions about which Processes and Worldview Scores apply to each segment.

  • Highlight specific text segments
  • Assert applicable Processes
  • Assert applicable Worldview Scores
  • Build a map of claims tied to policy text

Layperson Engagement

These four segments are designed for the average person. No specialized knowledge required. Simple swipe-based interactions make policy evaluation accessible to the general population.

1
Policy Sense Check Abstract Policy Evaluation

Action: Swipe on whether the policy (or parts of it) makes sense or not in the abstract.

Purpose: Gauge general public intuition about policy coherence before diving into detailed assertions.

2
Assertion Validation Worldview & Process Claims

Action: Review assertions of Worldview Scores and Processes applied to text segments. Swipe on whether the assertion makes sense or not.

Purpose: Crowdsource validation of expert-made assertions to surface questionable or strong claims.

3
Competing Assertions Group vs Group Scoring

Action: Choose between divisive and competing scoring assertions made by different groups.

Purpose: Surface where different worldviews diverge on interpreting the same policy text and let the public weigh in.

4
Policy Comparison Competing & Conflicting Policies

Action: Choose between divisive, competing, or logically conflicting policies in the abstract.

Purpose: Understand public preference when faced with mutually exclusive or tension-laden policy options.
