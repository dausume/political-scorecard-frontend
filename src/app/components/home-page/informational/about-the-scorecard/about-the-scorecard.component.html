About the Political Scorecard

A transparent, democratic framework for evaluating policies and solutions based on measurable outcomes and diverse worldviews.

What is the Political Scorecard?

The Political Scorecard is a platform designed to bring transparency, measurability, and democratic participation to policy evaluation. Rather than relying on partisan rhetoric or simplistic left-right classifications, the scorecard enables communities to define what matters to them and measure how well policies and solutions address those concerns.

At its core, the scorecard transforms abstract political debates into concrete, comparable metrics. It bridges the gap between expert analysis and public participation, ensuring that both domain expertise and democratic voice shape how we understand and evaluate policy.

Core Concepts

The scorecard is built on four foundational elements that work together to create a comprehensive evaluation system.

description Terms

Terms are the fundamental building blocks of the scoring system. Each term represents a measurable concept like "Labor Quality," "Environmental Impact," or "Economic Growth."

Terms can be contextualized with specific data sources, time periods, and geographic scope, allowing the same abstract concept to be grounded in real-world measurements.

how_to_vote Worldview Ballots

Worldview Ballots allow individuals and groups to express how much they value different terms. By assigning weights to terms, participants create a personalized scoring equation that reflects their priorities.

These ballots become the lens through which policies and solutions are evaluated—the same policy can score differently depending on whose worldview is applied.

category Political Categories

Political Categories organize terms, policies, and solutions into thematic areas like Healthcare, Economic Policy, Environmental, or Technology.

Users can prioritize categories important to them, helping filter and surface relevant content while enabling cross-cutting analysis of how policies affect multiple domains.

groups Groups

Political Groups bring together people with shared political perspectives to collaborate on worldview ballots and participate in elections together.

Professional Groups organize domain experts (economists, healthcare professionals, engineers) who contribute specialized knowledge to policy and solution evaluation.

Three Approaches to Scoring

The scorecard supports three distinct but interconnected approaches to evaluation, each serving different purposes and audiences.

gavel Policy Scoring Evaluating Legislation & Policy

Policy Scoring analyzes drafted legislation and formal policy proposals. Legal professionals and policy specialists annotate text segments, asserting which terms and processes apply to each part.

Key Features:

  • Expert annotation of policy text segments
  • Mapping assertions to worldview scores
  • Layperson validation through simple swipe interactions
  • Comparison of competing policy interpretations

Purpose: Create accountability by establishing what a policy intends to achieve and measuring it against those stated goals.

engineering Solution Scoring Analyzing Implementation Approaches

Solution Scoring focuses on the "how" of policy implementation. Domain experts in business, economics, and technology develop requirements and evaluate concrete solutions against abstract process needs.

Five Expert Phases:

  • Requirements Development - Define what solutions must address
  • Logical Equivalence - Map solutions to abstract requirements
  • Implications & Viability - Assess real-world feasibility
  • WorldView Score Assertions - Define success metrics
  • Solution Composition - Combine solutions into policy processes

Purpose: Bridge the gap between policy intent and practical implementation through rigorous process analysis.

ballot Worldview Elections Democratic Definition of Values

Worldview Elections allow communities to democratically define how terms should be weighted and measured. Through debate and voting, groups establish shared scoring equations.

Election Process:

  • Propose term weightings and measurement approaches
  • Debate interpretations with other participants
  • Vote on competing worldview definitions
  • Generate group consensus ballots

Purpose: Ensure that scoring reflects democratic input, not just expert opinion, while surfacing where worldviews genuinely diverge.

link Terms feed into Worldview Ballots

The terms defined in the system become the options that participants weight in their worldview ballots. As new terms are added and contextualized, they become available for inclusion in future ballots and elections.

link Worldview Elections produce Group Ballots

When political or professional groups conduct worldview elections, the results become official group ballots. These aggregated worldviews can then be used to score policies, showing how different communities would evaluate the same proposal.

link Solutions compose into Policy Processes

Individual solutions evaluated in Solution Scoring can be combined with custom logic chains to form complete policy implementation processes. These composed solutions provide the "how" that accompanies the "what" of policy text.

link Categories enable cross-cutting analysis

Political categories tag terms, policies, and solutions, enabling analysis across domains. A healthcare policy might also impact economic and environmental categories—the scorecard surfaces these connections rather than forcing single-issue thinking.

Why This Approach Matters

visibility

Transparency

Every score can be traced back to its component terms, weights, and data sources. No black boxes—understand exactly why a policy scores the way it does.

diversity_3

Pluralism

Different worldviews produce different scores, and that's the point. The scorecard doesn't impose a single "correct" evaluation—it reveals how values shape conclusions.

track_changes

Accountability

By establishing success metrics upfront, policies can be measured against their stated intentions. This creates a feedback loop for learning what actually works.

people

Participation

Expert analysis is essential, but so is democratic voice. The scorecard creates pathways for both specialists and the general public to contribute meaningfully.
